# Game Narrative Craft

## Write for the surface

| Surface | Budget | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Card / item flavor | 1 line, under ~15 words | Voice and world in a glance; no plot |
| Tooltip / UI | 1 sentence | Clarity beats color |
| Bark (combat, idle, hover) | 3-10 words | Write in sets of 5+ so repeats are rare |
| Event / encounter text | 40-120 words + choices | Setup, tension, a decision with a cost |
| Visual novel scene | Open length | Pace with beats; give the player something to do every few lines |
| Quest log | 2-4 sentences | State the goal, not the backstory |

## Tie story to systems

- A story fact is stronger when a mechanic expresses it (a cursed relic that costs max HP says more than a paragraph).
- Choices should spend a resource, change a stat, flip a flag, or alter tone the player can feel. Otherwise cut the choice.
- Keep mechanics-facing wording consistent with the game's keyword list. Never invent a keyword in prose.

## Write for repetition

- Run-based games: assume the player sees every common line 10+ times. Vary phrasing, shorten, and keep jokes out of lines that appear often.
- Gate memorable lines behind rare triggers.
- Callbacks reward long players; make them optional, never required to follow the story.

## Voice

- Give each character a verbal fingerprint: sentence length, favorite metaphors domain, what they never say.
- Test: remove the speaker label. Can you tell who is talking?

## Exposition

- Reveal through conflict and objects, not lectures. One new piece of lore per scene is plenty.
